\section{Conclusion}

We studied whether capabilities learned from individual obfuscation transforms
can be composed to support reasoning over stacked and previously unseen
obfuscations. Across eight models, pooled and consistency-based training perform
well on familiar transformation combinations but can lose capability outside
the training distribution, particularly on unseen obfuscation families and the
untrained reverse-reasoning task.

Parameter-space merging provides a different trade-off. By composing
independently trained specialists, it preserves robustness across these
out-of-distribution settings while requiring only a single adapter at inference.
These results suggest that semantics-preserving transformations provide
complementary training views of the same underlying computation, and that
composing their learned updates can retain program-reasoning capabilities that
are lost under monolithic adaptation. Evaluating this effect across additional
languages and substantially different held-out obfuscation families is the
primary direction for future work.
