\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{Held-out obfuscation family.}
Unseen-family generalization is evaluated through X1, a trainable sibling of
the quarantined H1 obfuscator. H1 was read exactly twice, a pilot and a final
pass under pre-registered hypotheses, and no method, hyperparameter or
checkpoint in this paper was selected on it. On six systems trained on neither
family, X1 and H1 accuracy agree at Pearson $r = 0.9992$ (mean absolute
difference 0.48 points), and adapters trained on X1 lose 0.08 points when
evaluated on H1. X1 therefore stands in for H1 in every experiment. This still
covers only one held-out mechanism family and may not generalize to
qualitatively different obfuscation mechanisms.

\textbf{Uneven condition coverage.}
Some transformations are not applicable to every program, so paired comparisons
are computed only on programs shared by the relevant conditions. The evaluated
program subset therefore varies across some experiments.

\textbf{Model and language scope.}
The study evaluates eight models, with the main experiments conducted in Python.
The JavaScript set provides a limited cross-language check but does not establish
generalization across programming languages.
