\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rqs}

Fine-tuning a code model on obfuscated programs raises its accuracy on those programs by roughly
nineteen points. Every question below asks what that number is made of. We structure the
investigation as one diagnosis applied twice: two tests of whether learned competence survives
leaving the distribution it was fit to, run first on the field's existing adaptation strategies and
then on our own.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{RQ1: Do standard adaptation methods generalise, or memorise?} \\
Real-world obfuscated programs, particularly in malware analysis, rarely employ a single
transformation in isolation. We commit to two tests before any method is on the table: whether
competence survives being \emph{stacked} with a transformation family the model has never seen,
and whether it survives the task being run \emph{backwards} (given a return value, produce a call
that yields it). We apply both to the three standard strategies --- inference-time routing,
weight-space model merging, and broad multi-transformation fine-tuning --- treated as baselines
rather than as proposals.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{RQ2: What is memorised?} \\
Given that the baselines fail both tests, this question locates what adaptation latches onto
instead, because that determines the remedy. Through complementary analyses --- reliance on
identifier cues, transferability between mechanisms within one obfuscation family, and the model's
confidence profile --- we test whether fine-tuning learns programmatic semantics or the surface
regularities a particular transformation leaves behind. The section ends by naming what to anchor
to.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{RQ3: Does anchoring to clean-code behaviour repair what adaptation breaks?} \\
The diagnosis in RQ2 names its own remedy: anchor the model to something a semantics-preserving
transformation cannot change, its own behaviour on the unobfuscated parent of each program. We
derive a paired-consistency objective from that requirement, isolate its mechanism by ablating the
teacher, and measure across eight models and four lineages whether it retains the benefits of broad
training while removing broad training's degradation on unseen families and on clean code --- and
on how many models it does so.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{RQ4: Does the anchored model generalise?} \\
Finally, we re-run RQ1's two tests on the anchored model and state the bound. On a divergence
ladder of stacks that contain an unseen transformation family, and on the reversed task, we ask
whether the objective instils a new capability to handle arbitrary unseen obfuscation, or whether
it acts strictly as a preservation mechanism that protects the teacher's existing competence from
being destroyed by surface-level change.
