\subsection{RQ1: What Merging Composes}
\label{sec:rq1}
The merge combines six specialists, each trained on one transformation. We ask whether its advantage
tracks those transformations.

It does not. On stacks of seen transformations the KL-consistency objective and the router system
exceed the merge by three to six points. The merge's advantage over pooled training is largest on the
held-out family, on stacks containing it, and on the reversed task. No arm was trained on any of
these three. We read this as evidence that merging composes general program-reasoning capability
rather than transformation-specific knowledge. The evidence is the location of the advantage; we do
not measure internal structure.

\subsubsection{Adaptation Exceeds Clean-Code Accuracy}
\label{sec:recovery}
We measure every arm against the untuned model's accuracy on \texttt{L0}, which is the accuracy
obfuscation removes.

Untuned models retain 75--92\,\% of their \texttt{L0} accuracy on single transformations, 61--83\,\%
on depth-2 stacks, and 47--63\,\% on the held-out family. Adapted models reading obfuscated programs
reach 132--195\,\%, 110--180\,\%, and 82--132\,\% on the same three groups. Across the merge,
KL-consistency and router arms, 78 of 84 model\,$\times$\,condition-group cells are at or above
100\,\%. Six of eight models exceed 100\,\% on the held-out family.

Fine-tuning on obfuscated code produces a model that reads obfuscated programs more accurately than
the untuned model reads unobfuscated ones, including on an obfuscator family absent from training.

The gain on clean code is not specific to merging. On \texttt{L0} itself the merge exceeds the
untuned model by $+14.79$ to $+22.81$ points, significant on every model where the untuned reading
clears the format gate. Against the clean-code LoRA, however, the merge differs by $-0.30$ to
$+1.38$ points and the interval contains zero on all eight models. Merging and clean-code tuning
reach the same accuracy on unobfuscated source; what separates them appears only once the input is
obfuscated.

In the backward direction 29 of 78 cells reach 100\,\%, and the merge is the closest arm. The effect
is specific to the trained direction.

\subsubsection{The Gain Does Not Come From the Clean-Code Specialist}
\label{sec:ablation}
The merge contains a specialist trained on unobfuscated source, so its clean-code result invites the
reading that the specialist supplies it. We test this directly by rebuilding the merge from
different ingredients while holding the operator, density, weights and rank fixed
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}): \emph{no-L0} drops the clean-code specialist, and \emph{ident} and
\emph{struct} keep three and two specialists from a single semantic view.

Forward, none of it matters. Dropping the clean-code specialist changes \texttt{L0} accuracy by
$-0.60$ to $+0.60$ points across four models, every interval containing zero, and cutting from five
specialists to two changes it by $-1.20$ to $+0.54$, again containing zero everywhere. A merge of two
structural specialists recovers as much clean-code accuracy as the full six. The gain is a property
of adapting to obfuscation, not of any particular ingredient and not of the number of ingredients.

The adapter geometry gives the mechanism (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}). Measured on the weights alone,
the clean-code specialist's mean cosine to the five obfuscation specialists matches the
within-family pairs on every model: the difference runs from $-0.021$ to $+0.007$ across seven
models, while absolute alignment varies widely between them, from $0.35$ to $0.75$. The clean-code
specialist occupies no direction the obfuscation specialists do not already span, so removing it
removes nothing. Identifier and structural specialists are more aligned with each other than across
the two families on all seven, so the family structure is real; it is simply much smaller than the
distance to orthogonality.

Backward, the ingredients do matter, and the contrast with the forward result is the finding. Cutting
to two specialists costs $4.19$, $8.98$ and $6.52$ points on the three models where the narrow arm
clears the format gate, all significant. Dropping only the clean-code specialist costs $1.38$ to
$3.47$ points on three of four models and is null on the fourth. Breadth of semantic view, not the
clean-code specialist, is what the untrained direction needs. On CodeLlama the narrow merge also
loses the answer contract outright, at a format-failure rate of $0.33$ and $0.72$ against the six-way
merge's $0.04$ and $0.06$; on Llama-3.1-8B it holds the contract at $0.10$ with nothing gated and
still loses $8.98$ points, so the deficit is accuracy rather than formatting.

\begin{takeaway}{RQ1}
The merge is not the strongest arm on the transformations its specialists were trained on, and its
advantage is largest where the surface is unfamiliar. Forward adaptation exceeds the untuned model's
clean-code accuracy on 78 of 84 cells, including on an unseen obfuscator family. The effect does not
appear in the untrained direction. It does not come from the merge's clean-code specialist: rebuilt
from two structural specialists alone, the merge recovers the same clean-code accuracy on four of
four models, and the specialist updates share directions that make this expected.
\end{takeaway}

\subsection{RQ2: Composing Capabilities for Stacked Obfuscation}
\label{sec:rq2}
All adapted arms improve on stacks of seen transformations. At depths two through four they differ
from one another by three to six points, against a gap to the untuned model of 10 to 24 points.
One-shot prompting changes accuracy by a few points, which indicates the failure is not one of answer
format.

The strategies separate outside the training distribution. On the held-out family, paired by program,
pooled training is below every arm assembled from the same rows on all eight models: by 2.5--4.6
points against the router system, significant on eight, and by 1.8--3.0 points against the merge,
significant on five. Pooled training is also below an adapter trained on unobfuscated code alone on
seven of eight models. Adding transformations to the training set reduced accuracy on a
transformation outside it.

The router system is the strongest forward arm. It requires all eight experts resident at inference
and a gate evaluation at every layer and token. Two of its experts, \texttt{S3} and \texttt{S4}, are
absent from the merge. These do not account for its advantage: measured against the merge it gains
$+2.8$ to $+5.3$ points on composites containing neither, and $+2.2$ to $+4.7$ on composites
containing one, on all eight models.

\begin{takeaway}{RQ2}
Capabilities learned from individual transformations compose. The composition method determines what
survives outside the training distribution. Pooling the data into a single adapter falls below every
arm built from the same rows on 8/8 models on the held-out family, and below a clean-code-only
adapter on 7/8. Routing and merging both carry the capability outside that distribution.
\end{takeaway}

\subsection{RQ3: Robustness and Generalisation of the Merged Capability}
\label{sec:rq3}
We evaluate the merge on three conditions outside those its specialists were built from.

\paragraph{Deeper stacks.} The merge retains 110--174\,\% of clean-code accuracy from depth two to
depth four on seen transformations. Its margin over pooled training does not decrease with depth.

\paragraph{Unseen obfuscator family.} The merge exceeds the untuned model on the held-out family and
the stacks containing it on all eight models, by 6.6--17.6 points, significant wherever the reference
is readable. Paired by program with a 2{,}000-resample program-clustered bootstrap, the router system exceeds the
merge in this condition by $+0.96$ to $+2.75$ points on seven of eight models, and by $+0.76$ on the
eighth with an interval containing zero. On the stacks containing the held-out family the same
comparison gives $+1.00$ to $+2.77$ points, significant on seven.

\paragraph{Reversed task.} No arm is trained in this direction. Pooled training falls below the
untuned model on four of the six models where it is readable, and KL-consistency on five of eight, by
up to 13.2 points. The merge exceeds the untuned model on seven of eight models by $+3.0$ to $+6.4$
points and is null on the eighth. Across the three regimes and eight models it is not significantly
below the untuned model in any cell. On an independent second seed the backward gain reproduces at
$+3.56$, $+3.34$, $+3.53$ and $+3.89$ points across two models.

That property depends on the breadth of the merge rather than on merging as such. The ablated merges
of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} do fall below the untuned model here: the structure-only merge by
$6.15$ and $8.01$ points on Llama-3.1-8B and Granite-3.1-8B, and the identifier-only merge by $8.18$
points on Granite-3.1-8B, all significant. Of the four ingredient sets only the six-way merge is
never significantly below the untuned model backward on any model.

\paragraph{What the backward replies contain.} Execution-graded accuracy pools three distinct
failures, so we separate them (Table~\ref{tab:bwddecomp}): exact-input recovery, adherence to the
answer contract, and \emph{direction locking}, the fraction of backward replies that are exactly the
gold forward answer. The last is the specific failure of a forward-trained arm asked to reverse:
it returns the output it was trained to predict. The untuned models sit at the floor, at $0.000$ on
seven of eight. Every single-adapter arm rises above it, to $0.25$ for pooled training on
StarCoder2-15B and $0.67$ for KL-consistency on CodeGemma-7B. The merge does not: it is at
$0.001$ or below on all eight models (Table~\ref{tab:dirlock}), the only arm to hold the untuned
floor everywhere. It also holds the answer contract best, at $0.91$--$0.97$ against pooled
training's $0.18$--$0.98$. The backward gain is therefore not a grading artefact of either
failure mode, and the arms that lose backward accuracy do so in part by answering the forward
question instead.

\paragraph{Unseen language.} Every adapter is trained on Python. We evaluate four of them on the
JavaScript corpus, forward only, against the untuned model's accuracy on clean JavaScript
(Table~\ref{tab:crosslang}). \textbf{Every adapted arm reading obfuscated JavaScript exceeds the
untuned model reading clean JavaScript}, on every condition group and all four models, by $+16.4$ to
$+30.8$ points, significant in all twelve comparisons. The clean-code recovery of
Section~\ref{sec:recovery} is therefore not a property of the training language.

Two restrictions apply. The held-out obfuscator family has no JavaScript variant, so the
unseen-family claim does not cross languages. And CruxEval-X and HumanEval-X are ports: 87 of the
168 JavaScript evaluation programs are the same programs, with the same arguments and outputs, as
Python training programs, and an adapter could reproduce a memorised pair on those rather than read
the JavaScript. All figures here use the remaining 81 programs. On the full set the apparent margins
are 7 to 14 points larger, which is what memorisation buys.

Unlike the unseen family, the merge holds no advantage here. Pooled training exceeds it by $+5.69$
and $+5.56$ points on two models and is null on the other two ($+0.75$, $-0.58$). A new language and
a new obfuscator are both unfamiliar surfaces, but only the latter is one the merge is better at.

\paragraph{Source of the limit.} We train one further adapter with pooled training's recipe on the
inverse prompt, and evaluate it in both directions. It exceeds the untuned model by $+10.30$ points
backward on CodeLlama-7B and $+5.18$ on Llama-3.1-8B, above every forward-trained arm. Inversion is
learnable, and the backward losses follow from forward training. The same adapter exceeds the untuned
model forward by $+9.63$ and $+7.19$ points. Training on input prediction improves output prediction;
training on output prediction reduces input prediction.

\begin{takeaway}{RQ3}
The merged capability survives increased composition depth, an unseen obfuscator family, and the
reversed task, and its clean-code recovery survives a change of programming language. It is the only
arm not significantly below the untuned model in any regime on any of eight models, and the backward gain replicates at a second seed. It is also the only arm that holds
the untuned direction-lock floor on all eight: the other arms answer the forward question when asked
to reverse, at rates up to $0.67$. It is not the strongest arm on
familiar surfaces or on the unseen family, and clean-code recovery does not extend to the untrained
direction. A backward-trained control attributes that limit to forward training rather than to the
task. The robustness is a property of the breadth of the merge: narrower merges of the same
operator fall significantly below the untuned model in this direction.
\end{takeaway}
