\subsection{Obfuscation taxonomy}
Single transforms (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}) fall into three families: identifier (L1b, L1r, L2),
structural (S1, S2, and S2's two halves S3 and S4) and encoding (X1 and its halves). Each is applied
to the clean parent and never stacked during training. H1 is the quarantined held-out obfuscator; its
evaluation budget was spent under a pre-registered two-pass discipline, and every unseen-family number
in this paper is measured on its trainable sibling X1, which reproduces H1's difficulty at
$r = 0.9992$ (mean absolute difference 0.48 points). Stacks are a separate
namespace built by applying parts to the parent in order; order matters, and no system is trained on
a stack. Ten stacks contain only seen transforms (six at depth two, four at depth three to four); six
contain the unseen family or one of its halves and are the stimulus RQ4 turns on.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Stacked composites.} Composites are a separate namespace from the ladder:
parts are applied to the clean parent left to right, and order matters, so \texttt{C\_L1r\_S1} and
\texttt{C\_S1\_L1r} are different conditions --- the second renames the state variables that
control-flow flattening introduces. We evaluate sixteen. Six are depth-2 stacks of seen transforms,
including an order-control pair and one positive control (\texttt{C\_S4\_S3}) that reconstructs
\texttt{S2} by composing its two halves, which is how we check that composition behaves as the
ladder predicts. Four go deeper on seen transforms: three at depth 3 and one at depth 4. The
remaining six contain the held-out family --- three at depth 2, two more using half of it
(arithmetic or strings alone), and one at depth 3 --- and are the conditions no arm was trained for
in any part. Each composite inflates program length, by 5$\times$ to 22$\times$ over the clean
parent, and a per-composite cap bounds that growth; coverage differs between them because
structural transforms decline some programs by design, so every paired contrast runs on the
programs common to the cells it compares, fixed before evaluation. The full per-composite
definitions and item counts are in \texttt{configs/conditions\_composite.yaml}.

\subsection{Systems}
Every system is a LoRA on the same base model, evaluated on still-obfuscated code. \emph{Clean LoRA}
(\texttt{tuned\_L0}) is tuned on unobfuscated programs and is the control throughout; one
\emph{specialist} per seen transform; \emph{breadth} (\texttt{mono\_all}) is one adapter trained on
the pooled data of all six training conditions --- the five seen transforms and clean code; \emph{family} (\texttt{tuned\_X1}) is trained on the unseen family's
sibling and is the ceiling for that family; it is reported as that ceiling only and is excluded from
the comparison tables, because on exactly the conditions that matter most it is the one arm for which
the held-out family is not held out. \emph{Routing} mixes the specialists with a learned gate
(\texttt{mole\_router}), its argmax, a fixed uniform gate or a gate frozen at random initialisation;
the last two separate what the mixture is worth from what the routing is worth. \emph{Merging}
combines the specialists in weight space (TIES, DARE-TIES, DARE-linear) or anchors the merge on the
clean adapter. The \emph{anchored} objective (\texttt{cons\_lam3}) is Eq.~\ref{eq:paired_consistency}
with $\lambda = 3$, a plateau from a sweep on the trainable grid: the teacher is a frozen clean-code-tuned
adapter reading the unobfuscated parent, the student never sees the parent, and the objective trains on
exactly the rows breadth trains on (26{,}841 rows, 1{,}257 steps for both); at $\lambda = 0$ it reduces
to breadth identically, which makes breadth the data-matched control. A one-shot in-context baseline
completes the set.

\subsection{Measurement}
Forward accuracy is strict normalised exact match on the produced output, with no containment
matching; the backward task is graded by executing the produced call against the program as the model was shown it --- the obfuscated variant, not the clean parent --- and accepting any arguments that return the gold value, which is what the prompt asks for. We also report the stricter rate at which the \emph{gold} arguments are recovered.
Every contrast is a paired difference on the program subset common to its cells, with a
program-clustered bootstrap of 2{,}000 resamples; bold intervals exclude zero. Decision rules were
pre-registered before submission and are reported by their verdicts even when inconclusive.

A cell whose format-failure rate exceeds 0.25 enters no mean, and is marked rather than hidden. Such a
cell is not always a broken prompt contract: decomposing the gated cells shows that many of them are
replies that are exactly the gold \emph{forward} answer --- on CodeGemma-7B's anchored arm 96\,\% of
them, on StarCoder2-15B's tuned arms 79--91\,\% --- which is the forward-locking effect of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, not a formatting failure. We therefore distinguish the two in every table:
$\ddagger$ marks a gated cell whose replies are predominantly the gold forward answer and $\dagger$ one
whose replies are genuinely unparseable. Gating removes cells non-randomly, concentrating in the
backward direction and on weaker models, so backward means rest on fewer cells than forward ones.
Untuned format failure measures adherence to the prompt contract, not capability, so screening base
models by it is unsound.
