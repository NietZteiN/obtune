\subsection{Behaviour Prediction Tasks and Grading}
\label{sec:tasks}
Both tasks are posed on the program \emph{as the model is shown it} --- the obfuscated variant, never
the clean parent --- and both are decoded greedily with a constrained, no-chain-of-thought answer
format. The two are graded by different procedures, which we state separately because the asymmetry
is not incidental: forward answers are unique and backward answers are not.

\paragraph{Output prediction (forward).} The prompt gives the program and a call, and asks for the
value the call returns. The reply is graded by normalised exact match against the gold value, which
is obtained by executing the \emph{clean parent} on the same arguments, so the reference is
independent of any transform. Normalisation is deliberately minimal and fixed in advance:
surrounding whitespace is stripped, a code fence or pair of backticks is unwrapped, and a single
trailing period is removed. No containment or substring matching is used --- an earlier audit of a
substring grader on this corpus found roughly $3\%$ false positives, for instance \texttt{927}
matching inside \texttt{9273}. Where the value parses as a literal in both the prediction and the
gold, comparison is structural with a floating-point tolerance of $10^{-6}$; otherwise it is on the
normalised strings. We additionally record \emph{raw exact match} --- byte equality after whitespace
stripping alone, with no normalisation --- so the sensitivity of any effect to the normalisation
rules can be checked.

\paragraph{Input prediction (backward).} The prompt gives the program and a return value, and asks
for a call that produces it. Grading is by \emph{execution}, not by matching: the predicted call is
run against the obfuscated program in the project's sandbox and is correct if the canonical
representation of its return value equals the gold value. Matching the gold \emph{arguments} is not
required, because a return value generally has many pre-images and finding a different valid input
is a success rather than an error. We nonetheless report the stricter rate at which the gold
arguments are recovered (\emph{exact-argument} accuracy) alongside the execution rate throughout,
because the two differ substantially and the gap is itself a finding
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).

Three details of the backward grader matter for reproduction. The reply must be a single call
\texttt{name(arg,\,\ldots)} whose arguments are literals; prose, expressions such as
\texttt{range(3)}, variable references and multi-line replies are recorded as format failures rather
than repaired, since hunting for a call inside prose would conceal exactly the failure mode we want
to measure. Arguments are literal-checked in the parent process before anything reaches the sandbox,
so an unparseable prediction is a format failure and never an execution error. And the function
\emph{name} before the parenthesis is not graded: under identifier renaming the entry point is
renamed and the prompt states the new name, so a model that writes the original name has still
identified the input; the call is executed against the item's true entry point.

\paragraph{Format failures.} Both graders report a format-failure rate, and the constrained answer
format is what makes that rate meaningful. A cell whose rate exceeds $0.25$ is excluded from every
mean and marked rather than hidden; Section~\ref{sec:setup} gives the two marks and what distinguishes
them.

\subsection{Scope of Transformations Studied}
Single transforms fall into three families (Section~\ref{sec:background}): \emph{identifier}
transforms (\texttt{L1b}, \texttt{L1r}, \texttt{L2}), \emph{structural} transforms (\texttt{S1},
\texttt{S2}, and \texttt{S2}'s two halves \texttt{S3} and \texttt{S4}), and an \emph{encoding} family
(\texttt{X1} and its halves) that is held out of all training. Each single transform is applied to
the clean parent and never stacked during training; stacks are a separate namespace, evaluated only.
Training is Python throughout. The JavaScript corpus transferred intact and is used for the
cross-language check of Section~\ref{sec:rq3}; it cannot be regenerated on our cluster, which fixes
it to the conditions it already contains (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

\subsection{Single Transformations}
Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} defines the single transforms. The identifier family destroys the evidence a
reader relies on and leaves the computation's shape intact; the structural family leaves identifiers
and changes the shape; the encoding family changes how values are represented. Within the structural
family, \texttt{S3} and \texttt{S4} are the two halves of \texttt{S2} --- one the reader must reason
about, one it can ignore --- and exist as separate transforms so that composition can be checked
against a known whole.

\subsection{Stacked Composites}
Composites are a separate namespace from the ladder:
parts are applied to the clean parent left to right, and order matters, so \texttt{C\_L1r\_S1} and
\texttt{C\_S1\_L1r} are different conditions --- the second renames the state variables that
control-flow flattening introduces. We evaluate sixteen. Six are depth-2 stacks of seen transforms,
including an order-control pair and one positive control (\texttt{C\_S4\_S3}) that reconstructs
\texttt{S2} by composing its two halves, which is how we check that composition behaves as the
ladder predicts. Four go deeper on seen transforms: three at depth 3 and one at depth 4. The
remaining six contain the held-out family --- three at depth 2, two more using half of it
(arithmetic or strings alone), and one at depth 3 --- and are the conditions no arm was trained for
in any part. Each composite inflates program length, by 5$\times$ to 22$\times$ over the clean
parent, and a per-composite cap bounds that growth; coverage differs between them because
structural transforms decline some programs by design, so every paired contrast runs on the
programs common to the cells it compares, fixed before evaluation. The full per-composite
definitions and item counts are in \texttt{configs/conditions\_composite.yaml}.


\subsection{Held-Out Family and Quarantine Protocol}
The paper's discriminator is an obfuscator family no arm is trained on. Its stimuli live under a
quarantine that no training path can read: the single training entry point refuses anything outside
the training tree or carrying the family's labels, a lint pass rejects raw reads that bypass it, the
generator that produces the family refuses to run without an explicit flag and writes read-only, and
a content scan over every training file checks for the family's markers regardless of labels. The
family itself, \texttt{H1}, was evaluated exactly twice --- one frozen pilot pass and one final pass
--- under a pre-registered discipline with every read logged, and its budget is spent.

Every unseen-family number in this paper is therefore measured on \texttt{X1}, a trainable sibling
built for exactly this purpose: it reproduces \texttt{H1}'s difficulty at $r = 0.9992$ (mean absolute
difference 0.48 points) on arms trained on neither, and transfer from \texttt{X1} to \texttt{H1} is
lossless (0.08 points) for arms trained on \texttt{X1}. No number from \texttt{H1} selects, tunes or
ranks anything.

\subsection{System Instantiation}
Section~\ref{sec:baselines} describes what each arm is; this is how it is instantiated. Every system
is a LoRA of rank 32 ($\alpha = 64$) on the same target modules of the same base model, evaluated on
still-obfuscated code, so the arms differ only in what they were trained on and how they are
combined.

\emph{Clean LoRA} (\texttt{tuned\_L0}) is trained on unobfuscated programs; one \emph{specialist}
per seen transform; \emph{breadth} (\texttt{mono\_all}) on the pooled data of all six training
conditions --- the five seen transforms and clean code --- 26{,}841 rows in 1{,}257 steps. The
\emph{KL-consistency} objective (\texttt{cons\_lam3}) is Eq.~\ref{eq:paired_consistency} at $\lambda = 3$,
a plateau from a sweep on the trainable grid, with a frozen clean-code-tuned adapter as teacher; the
student never sees the parent, and it trains on exactly breadth's rows and steps, so at $\lambda = 0$
it reduces to breadth identically. The \emph{router} (\texttt{mole\_router}) mixes eight specialists
with a learned gate, reported alongside its argmax, a fixed uniform gate and a gate frozen at random
initialisation.

\emph{Merges} combine the six adapters for $\{$\texttt{L0}, \texttt{L1b}, \texttt{L1r},
\texttt{L2}, \texttt{S1}, \texttt{S2}$\}$ --- precisely the conditions breadth pools --- with uniform
weights, density $0.5$, and sign election by summed signed magnitude. We report \textsc{TIES} and
\textsc{DARE-TIES}. Density was selected by a sweep over $\{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$ on a 1.5\,B pilot model
outside the panel and applied unchanged to all eight, so it is not fitted to the panel and not tuned
for it either. Merging is done in LoRA space (Section~\ref{sec:merging}) and the merged adapter is
written at the same rank, so it is served exactly as any other adapter is.

Two arms exist but are excluded from the comparison tables. \emph{Family} (\texttt{tuned\_X1}) is
trained on the held-out family's trainable sibling: it is the ceiling for that family and is reported
only as that, because on exactly the conditions that matter most it is the one arm for which the
held-out family is not held out. \emph{DARE-linear} is a third merge variant we ran and do not
discuss; it is in the appendix grids.


\subsection{Measurement and Reporting}
Every contrast is a paired difference on the program subset common to its cells, with a
program-clustered bootstrap of 2{,}000 resamples; bold intervals exclude zero. Decision rules were
pre-registered before submission and are reported by their verdicts even when inconclusive.
A cell whose format-failure rate exceeds 0.25 enters no mean, and is marked rather than hidden. Such a
cell is not always a broken prompt contract: decomposing the gated cells shows that many of them are
replies that are exactly the gold \emph{forward} answer --- on CodeGemma-7B's KL-consistency arm 96\,\% of
them, on StarCoder2-15B's tuned arms 79--91\,\% --- which is the forward-locking effect of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, not a formatting failure. We therefore distinguish the two in every table:
$\ddagger$ marks a gated cell whose replies are predominantly the gold forward answer and $\dagger$ one
whose replies are genuinely unparseable. Gating removes cells non-randomly, concentrating in the
backward direction and on weaker models, so backward means rest on fewer cells than forward ones.
Untuned format failure measures adherence to the prompt contract, not capability, so screening base
models by it is unsound.
