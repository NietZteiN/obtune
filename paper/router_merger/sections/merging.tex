We train one low-rank specialist per obfuscating transform and combine the specialists into a single
adapter before inference. The resulting system occupies the same design point as a router over
experts --- per-transform specialists, composed --- but resolves the composition offline, so the
deployed artefact is one adapter of the base architecture, requiring no gate, no expert bank, and no
additional computation at inference time.

\subsection{Task Vectors and Sign-Consensus Merging}
Let $W_0$ denote the frozen base weights of a target module. Specialist $i$, trained on transform
$i$, contributes the update $\Delta_i = B_i A_i$ with $B_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and
$A_i \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$; since the base is frozen, $\Delta_i$ is precisely the task vector
of that specialist in the sense of Ilharco et al.~\cite{taskarithmetic}. The objective of merging is a
single $\Delta^{\star}$ that retains the competence of all $n$ specialists.

Uniform averaging, $\Delta^{\star} = \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i \Delta_i$, degrades as $n$ grows because
entries on which specialists disagree cancel. TIES~\cite{ties} attributes this degradation to two
forms of interference and removes each in turn. First, each $\Delta_i$ is \emph{trimmed} to its
largest-magnitude entries, discarding updates that carry little signal. Second, a sign is
\emph{elected} for each parameter by summing signed magnitudes across specialists. Third, the merge
averages only those entries whose sign matches the elected one, so a specialist that opposes the
consensus on a parameter contributes nothing to it. DARE~\cite{dare} prepends an independent
sparsification: a fraction $p$ of each $\Delta_i$'s entries is dropped at random and the survivors are
rescaled by $1/(1-p)$, which preserves the update in expectation while reducing redundancy. Neither
procedure involves training; both are arithmetic on weights already learned.

\subsection{Application in LoRA Factor Space}
The published formulation of TIES operates on the assembled updates $\{\Delta_i\}$. The standard
implementation for LoRA adapters, which we adopt, instead applies the trim--elect--merge rule
independently to the set of $A$ factors and to the set of $B$ factors, forming
$\Delta^{\star} = B_{\mathrm{m}} A_{\mathrm{m}}$, where each specialist's combining weight $w_i$ is
split as $\sqrt{w_i}$ across the two sides so that their product recovers $w_i$.

These two procedures are not equivalent, and the inequivalence is structural rather than numerical.
Each entry of an update is a sum over the rank,
$\Delta_i[m,n] = \sum_{t=1}^{r} B_i[m,t]\,A_i[t,n]$, so no entry of $A_i$ or $B_i$ corresponds to a
single parameter of $\Delta_i$. The proposition ``the specialists agree on the sign of this
parameter'' therefore denotes a different quantity in each space. Figure~\ref{fig:factorspace}
contrasts the two. Empirically the gap is substantial: for random factors with $r = 4$, three
specialists, and density $0.5$, the outputs $B_{\mathrm{m}}A_{\mathrm{m}}$ and
$\mathrm{TIES}(\{B_iA_i\})$ exhibit a cosine similarity of $0.40$ and disagree in sign on $39\%$ of
entries.

We adopt factor-space merging because the alternative introduces a confound. Assembling each
$\Delta_i$, merging, and re-extracting a low-rank adapter requires a truncated SVD, which alters the
adapter's rank and introduces reconstruction error \emph{prior to} the evaluation of the merging rule;
the deployed adapter would then differ from the base architecture, and any measured effect would
combine the merge with its re-extraction. Factor-space merging preserves the rank exactly and yields
an artefact servable by the same multi-adapter path as any single specialist. The consequence for
interpretation is that our results characterise sign-consensus merging \emph{as it is applied to LoRA
adapters in practice}, rather than the published task-vector algorithm in its original setting.
Whether the two differ in downstream accuracy on our panel is an open question that the present
experiments do not address.

\subsection{Weighting}
Specialists are combined with uniform weights $w_i = 1/n$. Non-uniform weighting constitutes an
additional degree of freedom, and holding the weights uniform preserves the comparison against pooled
training --- which likewise weights its constituent conditions equally by construction --- as a
comparison of composition strategy rather than of tuning effort.
