

\section{RQ4: Is it robustness, or a better surface heuristic?}

We state our claims at their true strength: the paired-consistency objective generalises along every axis we can test except the one that would demonstrate genuinely new capability. 

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering

\toprule
\textbf{Stress Test} & \textbf{Result} & \textbf{Reading} \\
\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The anchored model handles deeper stacks smoothly, replicates well into JavaScript, and is the only SFT-family arm undamaged on the reverse task. However, on the unseen family, it merely equals the clean-code control. We are explicit in our conclusion: anchoring buys breadth's recombination benefit at no cost, but provides no new invariance. It is not magical robustness; it is simply a safer heuristic.
