\title{Does fine-tuning teach semantic invariance?\\
       Composition, anchoring, and what adaptation to obfuscated code actually learns}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{\institution{Under review}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning a code model on obfuscated programs raises its accuracy on those programs. We ask
whether that buys \emph{invariance} to meaning-preserving transformation or merely
\emph{memorisation} of the transforms seen in training, and we separate the two by never training
or evaluating on recovery: every evaluation is on code that stays obfuscated.

Across \textbf{eight models spanning four lineages} we find a dissociation that single-lineage
evaluation hides. Training on a \emph{breadth} of transforms composes on stacks built from those
same transforms, and pays a measurable tax on a held-out transform family --- a tax whose sign is
the same on every model we tested, with no contradiction. A paired-consistency objective that
anchors the model's answer distribution to its own behaviour on the unobfuscated parent removes
that tax on six of eight models, and \emph{reverses} on one. Its clean-code cost is likewise
model-dependent, holding on five of eight.

We then build the stimulus the question needs and that the literature lacks: stacks that contain a
transform family the model has never seen. Breadth's advantage flips sign the moment such a
component enters, while anchoring rises above the clean-code control. That flip is not uniform:
it is concentrated in stacks that leave the model no identifier surface to key on, which we
establish with a rule fixed before the stimulus existed. \slot{one-sentence takeaway once the
out-of-sample divergence reads complete}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Obfuscated code is a deployment condition, not a thought experiment. Malware triage, minified and
bundled production JavaScript, and plagiarism and clone detection all present models with programs
whose surface has been deliberately rewritten while their behaviour has not. Adapting a code model
to that condition works, and works easily: fine-tuning on output prediction over obfuscated programs
gains \textbf{18.09 points} on the transforms it was trained on. If that were the end of it, this
would be an engineering note.

What makes it a question is that real obfuscators \emph{stack} transforms. A production pipeline
renames identifiers \emph{and} flattens control flow \emph{and} inserts opaque predicates. So: does
single-transform competence compose? That is a question about what adaptation actually learned,
asked in a form that can be measured.

\textbf{The answer is a dissociation rather than a failure, and stating it as failure would be both
wrong and less interesting.} The same pair of adapters takes opposite signs on two axes. On stacks
built from transforms the model was trained on, breadth training beats clean-code tuning by
\textbf{+2.7 to +5.3 points} more than it does on stacks containing a family it never saw --- a gap
significant on \textbf{every one of the six models we could measure it on}, across four lineages
(Table~\ref{tab:dissoc}). Composition in the \emph{data} works; composition after the fact does
not. Inference-time routing over per-transform specialists beats a \emph{random} gate by
$+0.0000$, and weight-space merging lands at or below the clean-code control.

\textbf{Why.} Adaptation is anchored to the surface a transform leaves behind, not to the semantics
it preserves. The cleanest evidence is a family split in two: its arithmetic half and its
string-encoding half do not transfer to each other, yet either half alone recovers about 90\,\% of
the whole adapter's gain on the family. That is not additive, so it is not two skills being summed.
The second piece is direction specificity: asked backwards --- given a program and a return value,
produce a call that yields it --- the arm that gains $+18.09$ forwards \emph{loses} ground.

\textbf{What follows for method, and its bound.} If the failure is surface anchoring, anchor to
something else: the model's own behaviour on the unobfuscated parent of the same program. A
paired-consistency objective does exactly that, and it removes breadth's costs on most models we
tested. \textbf{We state the bound in the introduction rather than leaving a reviewer to extract
it}: on the held-out family the anchored model is as robust as clean-code tuning and \emph{no more},
its clean-code benefit holds on five of eight models, and on one model it reverses. The objective
buys breadth's recombination benefit at little cost; it does not add the capability breadth lacks.

The paper's separation from the DOBF lineage is a design decision rather than a framing: we never
train or evaluate on deobfuscation. Evaluation is always output prediction over code that remains
obfuscated, so what we measure is semantic competence \emph{under} transformation and not the
ability to undo it.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Condition ladder.} Conditions are \textbf{single-transform from the clean parent, never
stacked}, with identical semantics in Python and JavaScript: identifier-level (adversarial
renaming, random renaming, sequential minification) and structural (control-flow flattening, opaque
predicates with dead code). A held-out family --- string encoding plus guarded mixed
boolean-arithmetic rewriting --- is never trained on. Because its evaluation budget is spent, every
held-out number in this paper is measured on \textbf{X1}, a trainable sibling built from a
different surface scheme that reproduces the held-out family's difficulty level
($r=0.9992$).\footnote{The sibling's calibration and the quarantine discipline behind it are in the
supplement; no number here reads the quarantined family.}

\paragraph{Systems.} \textsc{clean} tunes on the unobfuscated parent only. \textsc{mono} tunes on
the breadth of all six ladder conditions. \textsc{cons} adds a paired-consistency term,
\[
\mathcal{L} = \mathrm{CE}(y \mid x_{\text{obf}})
  + \lambda\,\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_{T}(\cdot \mid x_{\text{parent}}) \,\|\, p_{S}(\cdot \mid x_{\text{obf}})\right)
\]
at answer tokens, where $T$ is a \emph{frozen clean-code-tuned} adapter reading the unobfuscated
parent of the same program and case. \textsc{fam} tunes on the held-out family's sibling.
\textbf{\textsc{mono} is the exact data-matched control for \textsc{cons}}: both train on the same
26{,}841 rows for the same 1{,}257 steps, and with $\lambda=0$ the objective reduces to
\textsc{mono} identically --- the student never sees the parent view, only the frozen teacher does.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Every contrast is a program-clustered bootstrap (2{,}000 resamples, seed
17); programs rather than items are the resampling unit because one program contributes several
correlated input cases. Intervals are 95\%. Coverage differs by condition, so every contrast runs
on a common subset of programs \textbf{recorded before any system is evaluated}.

\section{RQ1 --- Does single-transform adaptation compose?}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Composition in the data works, and only within what the data covered.} Breadth training
gains on stacks built from transforms it saw and does not on stacks containing a family it did not.

\section{RQ2 --- What is learned, and why it does not compose}
\label{sec:rq2}

Four instruments, sharing no machinery, point the same way.

\paragraph{1. The ladder cannot discriminate.} Mean off-diagonal transfer ratio is $0.906$
[$0.878$, $0.932$], and after BH-FDR correction \textbf{1 of 30} per-condition specialist cells
beats clean-code-only tuning. Whatever a specialist learns about its own transform, tuning on clean
code alone has largely already learned. The seen ladder is therefore not a test of invariance; only
an unseen family is, which is what motivates the held-out design in \S\ref{sec:setup}.

\paragraph{2. The family is not the unit.} Splitting the held-out family into its arithmetic and
string-encoding halves, neither half transfers to the other's condition ($+1.49$ [$-0.95$, $+3.93$]
and $-0.10$ [$-2.38$, $+2.19$]). Yet \textbf{either half alone recovers about 90\,\%} of the whole
adapter's gain on the full family ($+4.12$ / $+4.20$ against $+4.61$). That is not additive, so it
is not ``arithmetic skill plus string skill''. \emph{What it is instead we do not claim}: the
discriminating test --- scoring each half-adapter on programs containing \emph{none} of the
mechanism it trained on, the only place a shared-surface account can act alone --- returned
\textsc{undecided} on all three of its pre-registered rules.

\paragraph{3. Breadth polarises rather than degrades.} The breadth arm's log-probability of the gold
answer sits about 4 nats below the clean-code control on \emph{every} condition, and the entire
deficit lives in the items it gets \emph{wrong}: where it is right it is \emph{more} confident
($-0.16$ vs $-0.33$), where it is wrong it is roughly \emph{twice as far} from the gold ($-12.95$
vs $-7.00$). Sharpening is free on conditions it has seen and expensive on a family where its
decisions are more often wrong. This supplies the mechanism for the tax in
Table~\ref{tab:panel}, and the anchored arm sits on the clean-code arm's profile on both sides.

\paragraph{4. The competence is direction-specific.} The same adapters, the same programs, asked
backwards --- given a program and a return value, produce a call that yields it, graded by
execution. The arm that gains $+18.09$ forwards \emph{loses} $1.67$ [$-3.29$, $-0.04$] backwards.
At most a tenth of any forward gain survives the flip. \slot{panel-wide reverse-task numbers once
the eight-model inverse grids land}

\paragraph{Secondary, and weak by construction.} Tuning moves models \emph{away} from human
difficulty orderings ($\Delta\rho = -0.303$ [$-0.575$, $-0.028$]); the untuned model tracks the
human gradient at $+0.82$ and every tuned arm inverts it. One trial per cell over 98 cells, and the
one interval that clears zero does so at $-0.028$. The power ceiling is a property of the human
study's design and cannot be raised by compute.

Breadth's advantage on a stack depends on the stack containing an \textbf{identifier} transform.
We establish this as a difference rather than as an ordering, because one interval excluding zero
while another does not is not a test of the difference between them.

removing it would have made this pattern look \emph{cleaner} and be \emph{more wrong}.

\section{RQ4 --- Robustness, or a better surface heuristic?}
\label{sec:rq4}

Every composite stimulus in the prior literature, and in our own earlier work, is built from
transforms the model has seen. The question RQ4 asks cannot be answered with such a stimulus. We
build six composites that each contain a component from the held-out family, fix the program subset

\section{Threats to validity}
\paragraph{Single language.} Every headline is Python. The JavaScript corpus exists and the
cross-language replication runs on it, but the toolchain needed to \emph{regenerate} it is absent
on our cluster, so no new JavaScript condition could be built. We report the replication and do not
generalise beyond it.

\paragraph{The held-out family is a proxy.} Its evaluation budget was spent under a pre-registered
two-pass discipline, so every held-out number here is measured on a trainable sibling calibrated
against it ($r = 0.9992$, mean absolute difference 0.48 points, on arms trained on neither).
Transfer between them was lossless (0.08 points) for arms trained on the sibling. That calibration
is itself a result and is reported as one; it is not an assumption.

\paragraph{Coverage is not uniform, and contrasts are paired.} Structural transforms decline on
some programs by design and the held-out family requires a minimum number of sites, so conditions
cover different program sets. Every contrast in this paper runs on a subset recorded \emph{before}
any system was evaluated, and where two groups are compared the program set is intersected first.
Where that intersection moves an estimate, we report both.

\paragraph{Eight models is a small sample for a claim about models.} \S\ref{sec:rq3} declines to
make one. The lineage pattern there is reported as a methodological warning with its arithmetic
attached, because with $n=8$ and vendor confounded with lineage it cannot support more.

\paragraph{Instrument failures we report rather than omit.} The causal attention instrument is
inert: an identifier knockout moves gold log-probability by under 0.5\% for every arm on every
condition, and both of its pre-registered hypotheses were refuted. The attention account is
therefore correlational and is labelled as such.

\section{Related work}

We join three conversations and contribute a negative to one of them.

\paragraph{Deobfuscation.} A substantial line of work trains models to \emph{recover} obfuscated
code --- to undo renaming, to reconstruct identifiers, to normalise structure --- and measures
success by how close the recovery is to the original. Our separation is a design decision rather
than a framing: we never train or evaluate on recovery. Evaluation is always output prediction over
code that stays obfuscated, so what we measure is semantic competence \emph{under} transformation.
The distinction matters for the question, not only for the setup: a model that recovers well may do
so by pattern-matching the obfuscator, which is exactly the hypothesis we are trying to separate
from invariance.

\paragraph{Model merging and modular adaptation.} Task-vector arithmetic, TIES- and DARE-style
merging, mixtures of LoRA experts, and learned routing over adapters all propose that capabilities
trained separately can be combined after the fact. We contribute a negative with an unusually strong
control. Our router is compared not only against a uniform mixture but against a \emph{random}
gate, and on single transforms it beats that random gate by $+0.0000$ --- the mixture is worth
something and the routing is worth nothing. We also contribute a warning about the usual
explanation: merging's failure here is \emph{not} accompanied by the interference signature that is
supposed to cause it. Table~\ref{tab:geom} shows the adapters being merged are the most aligned and
least conflicting bank we measured, and that varying the random seed moves the update direction far
more than varying the task does. Any account of merging that reasons from task-vector geometry
should first check whether that geometry encodes the task at all. \slot{routing and merging on
stacked inputs, once those cells land}

\paragraph{Robustness to semantics-preserving transformation.} Work on adversarial and
semantics-preserving perturbation of code --- renaming, dead-code insertion, control-flow rewriting
--- generally asks whether a model's prediction survives the edit. We ask a sharper version: whether
\emph{adaptation} to such edits generalises past the edits it saw. Our answer is the dissociation in
Table~\ref{tab:dissoc}. Separately, our reverse-task result is a value-level replication of
direction specificity in code understanding: the same adapter that gains substantially at predicting
a program's output loses at predicting an input that produces a given output.
